## Introduction

"We just want a rough number." It is the first thing every client asks and the thing agencies are worst at answering. This guide publishes real Christchurch web design pricing for 2026 — what each tier actually gets you, what moves the price up or down, and the hidden costs most quotes leave out. ## The Real Price Ranges in 2026

### DIY site builders: $30–$80/month

Squarespace, Wix, Shopify Basic and their cousins. Right for: pre-startup validation, a club, a one-page CV business. The platform fee is just the start — budget real hours (20–60) to build something decent, plus ongoing time you will spend fighting templates instead of running your business.

### Template freelancers: $500–$2,500

A freelancer installs a theme, drops in your content, charges a flat fee. You get a working website quickly. The risks: template sites tend to be slow, poorly optimised for search, and indistinguishable from a hundred other sites. Fine for a business that needs *a* website. Not fine for a business that needs *customers* from a website.

### Small agency mid-tier: $3,000–$8,000

Custom design on a mainstream platform, mobile responsive, basic SEO setup, contact forms, and someone accountable when things break. This is the sensible band for most established small businesses — trades, hospitality, professional services — that need a site which looks credible and generates enquiries.

### Custom-built, performance-focused: $8,000–$25,000+

Modern frameworks (we build on Astro and similar), bespoke design, real SEO architecture, integrations with your business systems, sub-second load times, and a genuine conversion strategy. This band is where websites stop being brochures and start being revenue channels. For businesses where the website is the primary lead source, this tier typically pays for itself within the first year — our [web design process](/services/web-design/) is built around proving that with numbers.

### Ecommerce: $5,000–$30,000+

Online stores carry payment gateways, shipping rules, and product photography on top of everything above. Range depends on platform (see our [Shopify vs WooCommerce vs BigCommerce comparison](/blog/shopify-vs-woocommerce-vs-bigcommerce-nz/)) and catalogue complexity.

## What Actually Drives the Price

1. **Custom design vs template** — the single biggest cost factor. Custom means every screen is designed for your customers and your goals.
2. **Content** — who writes it, who sources photography. Professional copywriting typically adds $1,000–$3,000 and is the best money in the whole budget.
3. **Integrations** — booking systems, Xero, inventory, CRM connections. Each integration adds scope.
4. **SEO foundations** — technical setup, keyword mapping, on-page optimisation. Skipping this is how businesses buy a beautiful website nobody finds. See our [SEO services](/services/seo/) for what proper foundations include.
5. **Who builds it** — senior specialists cost more per hour and take fewer hours. Cheap hourly rates with slow delivery are the most expensive option on the market.

## The Hidden Costs Nobody Quotes

- **Hosting and maintenance** — $30–$200/month depending on platform and care level. Neglected WordPress sites are the most common expensive disaster we get called in to fix; our [maintenance plans](/services/website-maintenance/) exist because of that pattern.
- **Your own time** — content gathering, feedback rounds, image sourcing. Plan 10–20 hours of your involvement on even a small build.
- **The redesign in 3 years** — cheap builds get rebuilt; quality builds get refreshed. Amortise accordingly.

## How to Compare Quotes Fairly

Put two quotes side by side and check: Is design custom or template? Is SEO included or upsold? Who writes content? What are hosting/maintenance costs after launch? What happens when something breaks at 9pm on a Friday? The quote that looks 40% cheaper usually answers four of those five questions with silence.

Christchurch web design pricing in 2026 runs from about $500 to $25,000+, and the spread is not arbitrary — it tracks customisation, conversion strategy, and accountability.
