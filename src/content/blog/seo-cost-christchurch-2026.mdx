## Introduction

If you have ever searched for SEO pricing in New Zealand, you will have noticed something: almost nobody publishes their numbers. That is not an accident — opaque pricing makes it hard to compare providers. This guide fixes that for Christchurch businesses. Here is what SEO actually costs in 2026, what you should expect at each price point, and how to work out whether it is worth it for your business.

## The Three Price Bands

### Freelancers and part-time consultants: $300–$800/month

At the low end, solo consultants and moonlighters offer SEO from around $300 a month. For a very small local business with an already-solid website, this can be enough for the basics: Google Business Profile tuning, citation clean-up, and on-page fixes. The risk is capacity — one person means one set of hands, and SEO done at two hours a month rarely moves competitive keywords.

### Budget agencies: $800–$2,000/month

The middle market — and where most Christchurch businesses land. At this level you should expect monthly keyword research, on-page optimisation across your key pages, content production, citation management, and a monthly report. Quality varies enormously here. The question to ask any agency in this band: *who exactly is doing the work, and can you show me samples?*

### Specialist studios: $2,000–$5,000+/month

At the top end you are paying for strategy plus execution: technical SEO on complex sites, content engineered around search intent, digital PR for backlinks, and conversion tracking tied to revenue. This band makes sense when SEO is your primary growth channel, or when your average customer value is high enough that a handful of extra enquiries per month changes the business.

## What Drives the Price Up (and Down)

- **Competition in your niche** — ranking a plumber across all of Christchurch costs more than ranking a pool builder in Rolleston. Suburb-level targeting is the cheapest genuine advantage available to local businesses; we cover how it works in our [suburb SEO targeting guide](/blog/christchurch-suburb-seo-targeting-guide/).
- **The state of your website** — a slow, broken, or unindexed site needs foundation work before rankings are even possible. Fixing that is a project, not a retainer line item.
- **Content volume** — two optimised posts a month costs less than eight. More is not automatically better; consistency beats volume.
- **Link building** — genuine NZ backlinks (industry associations, local media, sponsorships) take real outreach time. This is the biggest cost differentiator between bands.

## Red Flags in SEO Pricing

- **Guaranteed #1 rankings.** Nobody can guarantee what Google's algorithm will do. Anyone promising it is either lying or using techniques that get sites penalised.
- **Suspiciously cheap "SEO packages"** — $200 a month usually means automated directory spam, which can actively harm your site.
- **Long contracts with no exit.** Serious agencies earn retention with results. Twelve-month lock-ins with no performance milestones are a warning sign.
- **No reporting.** If you cannot see what was done and what it achieved each month, you are not buying SEO — you are funding it.

## How to Work Out What SEO Is Worth to You

Do the maths backwards: what is a new customer worth per year? If a customer is worth $2,000 and SEO brings you four extra customers a month, even the top price band pays for itself several times over. ## A Note on Timelines

SEO is not a light switch. Expect three to six months before meaningful movement on competitive terms, with results compounding after that. Anyone selling you month-one page-one results is selling you something other than SEO. The upside of that same lag: the rankings you build become a moat your competitors cannot buy their way past quickly.
