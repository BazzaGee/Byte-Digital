## Introduction

It is the most common question we hear from Christchurch business owners: "Should I do SEO or Google Ads?" It is asked like an either/or choice, which is the first misconception. The honest answer depends on your timeline, margins, and competition — and the best answer for most businesses is a sequence, not a selection. Here is the framework we use with clients.

## The 30-Second Answer

- **Need leads this month?** Google Ads.
- **Building a business that compounds?** SEO.
- **Sensible default for most NZ small businesses?** Start with Ads while you build SEO — then let SEO take over the keywords where it wins.

## Speed: Ads Win, Every Time

A well-built Google Ads campaign can generate enquiries within days. SEO needs three to six months before meaningful movement on competitive terms. If you have a pipeline problem right now — a new business, a quiet winter, a new service launch — paid search buys you immediate visibility at the exact moment someone searches for what you sell.

## Cost Structure: Opposite Shapes

Google Ads is a rental: you pay for every click, forever. Stop paying, disappear instantly. In competitive NZ niches, clicks run $2–$10+, so a modest $1,500/month budget might buy 200–500 visits.

SEO is construction: the cost is front-loaded into optimisation and content, but the traffic it earns carries no per-click charge. A page that ranks on page one can deliver that same traffic every month at zero marginal cost. The catch: you pay before you earn, and results lag.

## Durability and the Moat Effect

Ads stop the moment your budget does. Rankings, once earned, are sticky — they decay slowly and resist new competitors. This is why SEO compounds: every optimised page and earned backlink makes the next one easier. Think of Ads as buying fish and SEO as building a greenhouse.

## What Each One Is Actually Good At

**Google Ads excels at:**
- Time-sensitive offers and launches
- Testing which keywords and messages convert *before* investing months of SEO
- Highly competitive head terms where organic ranking would take years
- Remarketing to people who visited your site

**SEO excels at:**
- "Near me" and suburb searches where buyers have high intent — this is where [local SEO](/services/local-seo/) pays its bills
- Building trust: organic results carry implied endorsement that ads do not
- Content that answers questions and captures early-stage buyers
- Long-term cost per lead that paid channels cannot match

## The Data Most Businesses Miss

In our work with Christchurch businesses, the pattern is consistent: paid and organic presence together outperform either alone. Searchers who see your ad AND your organic listing convert at higher rates — the double impression signals an established, credible business. Counter-intuitively, running Ads often *improves* your SEO performance by revealing exactly which keywords convert, so your organic efforts target proven demand.

## The Sequencing Strategy That Works

1. **Months 1–2:** Launch tightly-targeted Ads on your highest-intent keywords. Simultaneously fix technical SEO basics — page speed, indexing, [conversion-focused pages](/services/web-design/).
2. **Months 2–4:** Use Ads data to identify which keywords produce customers, not just clicks. Start SEO content targeting the proven winners, plus suburb-level terms competitors ignore.
3. **Months 4–12:** As pages rank, shift budget from Ads to the keywords where organic presence is now strong. Keep Ads for terms that remain too competitive and for remarketing.
4. **Steady state:** A blended channel mix where each enquiry source has a known cost, and total dependence on neither.

## Common Mistakes

- **Running Ads to a website that cannot convert.** Paid traffic to a slow, confusing site is a bonfire of money. Fix the landing experience first — our [digital marketing services](/services/digital-marketing/) always start there.
- **Quitting SEO at month three.** That is precisely when foundations are laid and momentum is invisible. Quitting then is like demolishing the house because the roof has not been painted.
- **Treating them as competing budgets.** The data each generates feeds the other. Run as rivals, both underperform.

## Conclusion

SEO versus Google Ads is the wrong fight. The right question is: what sequence gets my business the fastest path to a profitable cost per lead? For most New Zealand small businesses that means Ads first for immediate data and pipeline, SEO underneath for the compounding asset. Run both deliberately and they make each other cheaper.
